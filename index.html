// Philosophy

How I Approach Work

Principles that guide how I think about building and securing systems — learned from real failures, operational constraints, and years of hands-on work.

Small decisions compound

Most system failures aren't dramatic explosions — they're the accumulated weight of hundreds of small technical choices made consistently well. I pay attention to the details that accumulate into reliability or risk over time.

Clarity is infrastructure

Whether it's code, documentation, or an interface someone has to use at 3am during an incident, clarity matters. Good systems communicate what they're doing and why. Complexity should serve a purpose, not hide confusion.

Build for the long term

Real systems outlive their initial requirements. I design with that in mind — making tradeoffs that account for scale, change, and the people who'll maintain what I build. Good architecture ages gracefully.

Defense in depth, thoughtfully applied

Security isn't a checklist. It's about understanding how systems fail, where attackers look, and building layered protections that make sense for the actual threats and constraints. Every environment is different.
